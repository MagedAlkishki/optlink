A consumer data marketplace built on real, provable consent

People opt in and can withdraw at any time. Companies only ever see a masked match until they subscribe — and the moment they reveal a real contact, that access is written to a permanent, queryable audit log. This is the piece most scraped-data platforms skip entirely.

Opt-in by design

Every listed consumer explicitly consented, with a timestamp. Withdrawing consent removes them from all future search results immediately — the record just isn't deleted outright, so the consent history stays provable.

Nothing revealed for free

Browsing is always possible so a company can see whether a match exists, but full contact details only unlock for companies with an active subscription.

Every access is logged

The instant a company reveals a consumer's real email or phone, an access-log row is written — who looked, at whom, and when. A company can review its own history at any time.

Why this exists

This project started as a real business idea — a marketplace where people sell access to their own data to interested businesses. Researching Egypt's Personal Data Protection Law and GDPR made clear that running it live would mean government licensing and real compliance obligations before a single real user could sign up. Rather than shelve the idea, it's rebuilt here as a demo: every consumer and company below is invented, so there's no real personal data involved at all — just the architecture a compliant version would need, built and working.

Under the hood

Java 21 · Spring Boot 3 · Spring Data JPA · Bean Validation · H2 in-memory database · a REST API this page and the portal call directly.

Explore the API: GET /api/consumers/search?companyId=1, GET /api/consumers/count, GET /api/companies/1/access-log.
